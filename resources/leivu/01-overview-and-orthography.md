# Leivu: Overview and Orthography

## Identity & Heritage Enclave
- **Language code:** `leivu` (Leivu; historic South Estonian language island around Lejasciems, Ilzene, Alūksne, and Kalncempji in northeastern Latvia).
- **Subgrouping:** Archaic South Estonian enclave shaped by centuries of close contact with Latvian (including Latvian-like broken-tone prosody documented in *Balodis* & *JEFUL 2021*) while preserving distinctively South Estonian lexicon (`peni`, `laib`, `uma`, `om`) and morphology (`-n` inessive, `-st` translative).
- **MCP Support:** Over 2,100 dictionary entries (`eki_ems_dictionary`, `eki_vms_dictionary`, `leivu_eestijuured_vocabulary`, `leivu_esttyp_vocabulary`, plus Latvian/Estonian `dictionary_pairs`), scholarly corpus examples (`jeful_2021_leivu_examples`, `balodis_broken_tone_leivu_examples`, `leivu_native`, `leivu_academic_examples`), offline classifier (`leivu`), cross-dialect cognates, and Leivu leakage linter.

## Transcription & Phonological Features in Texts
1. **Morphological Segmentation Hyphens (`-`):** Academic Leivu examples frequently segment suffixes with hyphens for linguistic clarity (e.g., `vanu-n aju-n` "in old times", `magusa-mb` "sweeter", `aastagu-t` "years (part.)", `nuorõ-b` "younger"). Treat hyphenated endings as grammatical suffixes, not typos.
2. **Vowel Breaking / Diphthongization:** Long mid vowels show characteristic rising/breaking diphthongs (`uo` as in `nuorõ-b`, `ie` as in `piehmest` = *pehmeks*), plus `laib` (*leib*).
3. **Palatalization & Glottal/Broken-Tone Notation:**
   - Acute accent or apostrophe marks palatalization (`kut́sa`, `nińń`, `d́alan`, `d́ama`, `` `jalkši ``).
   - Backtick `` ` `` marks overlong/broken tone (`\`jahtama`, `\`kiitmise \`piehmest`).
   - Final `-q` or `’` may appear inconsistently (`eiq`, `eimu’`) or be reduced/apocopated depending on source tradition; never alter primary transcriptions.
4. **Latvian Lexical/Syntactic Contact:** Conjunction **`un`** ("and"), temporal **`nu`** ("now" alongside `põret` / `põrõda`), and voiced stops (`gusla`, `d́alan`, `d́ama`) appear naturally in authentic Leivu texts.
